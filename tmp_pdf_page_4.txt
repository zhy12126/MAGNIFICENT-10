A challenge for Beijing is that rapid RMB appreciation helps the second and third goals, 
but potentially undermines the first, as it would eventually cut into China’s international 
competitiveness, slowing exports and real GDP growth. But a moderate pace of 
appreciation – perhaps 3-5% a year against the dollar – would not impede ongoing 
market share gains, in our view. 
Economists typically think about the real trade-weighted exchange rate (TWI) as the 
most relevant in terms of international competitiveness, as it encompasses the range of 
trading partners as well as differences in inflation across countries. If gradual enough, 
RMB appreciation against the USD (i.e. declining USDCNY) need not erode 
competitiveness as measured by the real TWI, for two reasons. 
The USD is modestly overvalued by our assessment (with most of this overvaluation 1.

forecasts imply roughly a 1% annual depreciation in the broad USD TWI over the

appreciate at the same pace against the USD without appreciating against other 
currencies (on average).
