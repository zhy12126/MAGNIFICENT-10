China’s goods trade surplus continues to grow on the back of strong n
manufacturing competitiveness and technological progress, raising concerns 
about de-industrialization in other economies. Foreign policymakers are 
exploring a range of potential responses, including trade barriers and pressure 
for appreciation of the Chinese renminbi. 
Beijing’s stated economic policy objectives include further growth in high-tech n
sectors to “build a manufacturing superpower”, “self-reliance” in key inputs and 
technologies, and increased internationalization of the renminbi. We anticipate 
that China’s policymakers will continue to favor gradual CNY appreciation against 
the US dollar (3-5%/year) as the option that best balances these objectives. 
We expect both incremental broad USD depreciation in coming years and lower n
inflation in China than most other countries. Under these conditions, the 
renminbi could appreciate gradually against the dollar on a sustained basis 
without major impact on Chinese manufacturers’ relative competitiveness (as 
measured by the real trade-weighted exchange rate) or loss of global 
manufacturing share.
