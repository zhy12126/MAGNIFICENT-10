From the perspective of China’s household welfare and global imbalances, a better 
policy option would be more rapid appreciation coupled with greater support to 
domestic consumption. To the extent that currency appreciation slows headline GDP 
growth and introduces more deflationary pressures, it would incentivize Chinese 
policymakers to implement demand-boosting reflationary policies, which is another 
necessary condition for restoring economic balances for China. China might hold or gain 
global manufacturing share even in this scenario – for example, in the 2005-15 period, 
China’s share of global manufacturing grew rapidly even as its trade-weighted exchange 
rate appreciated, reflecting its strong competitiveness and the tailwind from WTO 
admission (Exhibit 6). Collectively, these actions would increase households’ purchasing 
power, and reduce global imbalances and trade tensions. Despite some calls by Chinese 
academics and former policymakers to support households, and the rollout of a 
five-year plan to improve consumption, Beijing appears to be moving very cautiously in 
terms of implementation. While the RMB appreciated fairly quickly in late 2025 and 
early this year (Exhibit 7), some of this may have reflected efforts to manage the 
relationship with the US, and recent signals from the daily fixing suggest Beijing would 
prefer to slow the pace going forward (Exhibit 8). 
 
Therefore, we continue to expect modest, low-to-mid-single-digit appreciation of the 
RMB in the coming years. Shocks that could change the path include a re-ignition of 
large-scale tariff war, or a major global economic downturn, either of which would take 
away the export impulse to China. Absent those, we expect USDCNY to reach about 6 at 
end-2028 and 5.50 at end-2031. Strength in the renminbi should also be supportive of 
other Asian currencies; correlations between CNY and other Asian currencies have risen 
in recent years. 
Key near-term events for the bilateral exchange rate are likely to include the US 
employment report and September Fed meeting, but most importantly the scheduled 
visit of President Xi to Washington to meet with President Trump on September 24.
